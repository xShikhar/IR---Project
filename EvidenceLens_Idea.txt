EVIDENCELENS - PROJECT IDEA FOR TEAM DISCUSSION

THE IDEA
Build a search tool that helps users find research evidence for a scientific
claim. Instead of only showing paper titles, it highlights the exact candidate
evidence sentences inside research abstracts and explains why they were ranked.

EXAMPLE EXPERIENCE
A user enters a claim such as "Treatment X reduces the risk of condition Y."
The tool finds relevant papers, highlights passages discussing that relationship,
and lets the user mark papers as useful or irrelevant to improve the search.
The user can compare keyword, semantic, and combined search results side by side.

HOW IT WORKS
1. BM25 searches for matching words, with separate title and abstract weights.
2. Semantic search finds related evidence even when the wording is different.
3. Reciprocal Rank Fusion combines the two result lists.
4. Sentence retrieval identifies candidate evidence within the selected papers.
5. An adaptive policy examines more candidates when retrieval signals are weak
   or disagree, and fewer when the signals are strong.
6. User relevance feedback can refine the query using the Rocchio algorithm.
   A pretrained cross-encoder reranker is an additional option if setup permits.

WHAT MAKES OUR VERSION INTERESTING
Our proposed contribution is an adaptive search policy that uses agreement
between retrieval methods AND sentence evidence signals to decide how much
reranking work to perform. We will test whether this saves computation while
preserving evidence recall. These are retrieval signals, not proof of truth.
The algorithms and scientific evidence-search task already exist; our claim is
about the particular policy, integration, and measured results, not inventing
scientific fact-checking or adaptive retrieval.

DATASET AND EVALUATION
Use SciFact: approximately 1,400 expert-written claims and 5,183 abstracts,
with annotated evidence sentences and support/refutation labels.
Compare BM25, semantic search, fusion, and fixed versus adaptive reranking.
Measure Precision@k, Recall@k, MRR, nDCG, sentence-selection precision/recall/F1,
latency, and candidates reranked. Keep evaluation labels out of runtime search.
Use a documented split of public labels; official test labels are hidden.

DEMO AND SCOPE
Core demo: enter a claim -> inspect ranked papers and highlighted evidence ->
compare retrieval methods -> view ranking explanations and evaluation charts.
Prioritize the working retrieval pipeline, adaptive policy, and comparison UI.
Add feedback and reranking as time permits. Automatic support/refute prediction
is a stretch feature requiring a validated model; similarity cannot supply it.
A focused prototype is the 2-3 hour target; experiments and report verification
need additional time. Improvements must be measured rather than promised.

WHY CHOOSE THIS OVER EXTENDING TURNTRACE?
It gives us a fresh application, public expert evidence annotations, visible
new interactions, and a focused research question for the report and viva.
